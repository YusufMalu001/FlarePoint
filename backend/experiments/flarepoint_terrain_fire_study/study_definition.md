# Terrain-Associated Patterns in Satellite-Detected Wildfire Activity: A Multi-Scale Geospatial Analysis

## Primary Research Question
How does the spatial distribution of satellite-detected wildfire activity vary across terrain characteristics (elevation, slope, aspect, TRI, TPI, curvature), and how do these associations change across spatial scales (`250 m`, `500 m`, `1 km`, `3 km`)?

## Exploratory Hypotheses
- **H1**: Satellite-detected wildfire activity is not uniformly distributed across elevation classes.
- **H2**: Detection distribution differs across slope classes.
- **H3**: Detection distribution varies across aspect sectors on sufficiently sloped terrain (`slope >= 5°`).
- **H4**: Observed detection density varies with terrain ruggedness (TRI).
- **H5**: Terrain associations vary depending on the spatial neighborhood scale used to characterize terrain.

## Scientific Guardrails
- This is an **observational study**; no causal claims are made.
- Satellite hotspot counts do **not** measure wildfire probability.
- Area-normalized density does **not** eliminate satellite observation opportunity or cloud bias.
